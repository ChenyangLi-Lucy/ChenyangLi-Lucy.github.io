ReadyToTalk

Bring the phone closer to start speaking. Keep it near through pauses. Move it away to finish.

Experiments · Data collection & collection software · Data analysis · Paper writing

In collaboration with ByteDance

The interaction

  1. 01

    Approach

    Bring the phone toward your mouth to open the voice channel.

  2. 02

    Hold

    Keep it nearby while speaking or pausing to think.

  3. 03

    Move away

    Lower the phone to explicitly end the interaction.

problem

A pause in speech does not always mean the speaker is finished. Voice interfaces that use silence to end input can cut people off while they are still thinking or holding the phone near their mouth.

solution

ReadyToTalk uses physical engagement to control voice input: bring the phone closer to begin, hold it near through pauses, and move it away to finish.

IMU, camera and echo signals help distinguish these gestures from ordinary phone handling.

Study 1 · User exploration

10 smartphone voice users described and demonstrated starting, holding, and ending voice interactions.

Checking a screen can resemble preparing to speak. These look-alike actions shaped the negative examples in the later dataset; pauses while holding the phone near the mouth shaped the hold state.

Study 2 · Comparative user experience

Study setup

12 participants · 3 conditions
ReadyToTalk, breath wake-up, and manual hold-to-talk, tested in counterbalanced order.

Short commands tested starts and stops. Longer speech tested pauses.

Wizard-of-Oz control
An experimenter changed the states to isolate the interaction from recognition errors.

Reading the results

NASA-TLX and 7-point ratings captured workload and interaction experience after each condition.

Ratings favored ReadyToTalk most clearly over breath wake-up. Participants valued staying active through pauses and wanted immediate confirmation when listening starts.

Experience, then sensing
Recognition was evaluated separately with a different participant group.

Multimodal data collection

49 participants · 18 tasks · 2,646 trials

A separate participant group performed scripted phone-use tasks. IMU, camera, echo, and event markers were synchronized into one record for each trial.

Prototype & collection software

The smartphone-like prototype collects synchronized sensing streams. The collection software selects scripted tasks, monitors sensors, and records START, Moving_Away, and END event markers.

Algorithm overview

The IMU proposes candidate motion intervals; camera and echo provide additional evidence for approach and move-away detection.

Sensing results

Selected IMU, camera, and echo fusion reached 93.35% approach-detection accuracy. The five-frame IMU and camera configuration reached 85.61% move-away accuracy in offline, user-independent evaluation.

Discussion & future work

Activation, holding through pauses, and termination form one connected interaction. The next step is an integrated, everyday-use evaluation of first-word capture, false triggers, latency, and battery cost on a production phone.
