PneuBrace

Pneumatic guidance, posture sensing and audio prompts for therapist-taught exercises at home.

Project Lead · Hardware · Experiments · Paper writing
IMWUT · Major revision

Tsinghua PI Lab × West China Hospital, Chengdu

problem

Adolescents practising scoliosis exercises at home can struggle to reproduce a posture taught in the clinic. Without the physiotherapist’s hands-on guidance, knowing that a posture is off does not explain how to correct it.

solution

PneuBrace combines inflatable chambers, posture sensing and audio prompts. Pressure gives a bodily reference for where to move; audio helps guide the timing.

The wearer actively performs the correction, using a target established with a physiotherapist.

Formative study

Five families and two physiotherapists explored candidate feedback. Their responses favored pneumatic pressure as a bodily reference and voice prompts for timing.

Touch as instruction

Provide a spatial cue that helps the wearer organize a movement.

Clear timing

Use voice to mark transition, holding, and recovery.

Active self-correction

Sense progress while keeping the wearer responsible for the movement.

PneuBrace system

Pressure provides a spatial reference, audio guides timing, and the IMU advances exercise states against a clinician-calibrated target.

Waistband textile screening

Comparing five candidate textiles

Five materials were screened for extensibility and waistband stability. The samples and force–elongation curves show their responses as they stretch.

The screening informed material selection for the waistband. It was a prototype material test, not a participant outcome.

Calibration & repeatable wearing

From the clinic to home practice

A physiotherapist establishes the target posture, cueing side, comfortable pressure, and belt position.

Scale markings help reproduce the setup at home. The annotated front and back views locate the IMU, airbag, tubing, controls, and waist pouch.

Device characterization

A 14-day field study

20 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis

Ten adolescents used PneuBrace and ten followed conventional home practice. Both groups completed daily 8–10-minute sessions, with baseline calibration and a Day 15 assessment.

Results

All 20 participants completed the protocol. The posture comparison was exploratory (p = .064); the sEMG and expert ratings describe short-term task execution. Interviews highlighted bodily guidance, prompt timing, and the practical demands of home use.

Discussion & next steps

The study points to instructional touch as a useful design direction. Future work needs longer deployments, improved wearability and calibration, and clearer feedback about what the device senses. Long-term clinical outcomes were not established.
